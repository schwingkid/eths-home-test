Parents assets

The parent page remains one continuous page. The dropdown is only a set of bookmarks into these substantial sections.

WHY YTC

The goal is bigger than the technology.

Computers, robotics, fabrication and engineering give students something concrete to master. The deeper model is the handoff: learn from someone older, teach someone younger, then grow into mentoring and leadership.

LearnBuild a real skill.
TeachExplain it to someone else.
MentorHelp another student succeed.
LeadOwn responsibility for the work.
THE EXPERIENCE

What students actually do.

The experience is part of understanding the club: what comes into the room, what students do with it, and where that can lead.

Learn by doing.

Students work with computers, robotics, fabrication, coding, troubleshooting and other practical technology.

Teach it forward.

Students turn what they know into lessons, mentoring and community teaching.

Meet a bigger world.

University and international relationships can bring instructors, peers and new perspectives into the ETHS clubroom.

ABOUT THE CLUB

Local roots. A much wider horizon.

YTC at ETHS is built around a simple cycle: students learn, teach, lead and train the next group. The wider YTC network matters because it creates more people to learn from, more people to teach, and more serious opportunities for students to grow.

What parents should know: students can enter at different skill levels. Advancement comes from growth, reliability and what a student can actually be trusted to do.

Evanston assets

The Evanston page is one story: local work first, student experiences farther from home, then the wider world coming back into ETHS.

LOCAL WORKS

The club turns outward.

Students use what they know — teaching younger students, helping people with technology, rebuilding equipment, and taking on work that makes them useful to someone else.

Teach younger students.

Learning becomes more serious when another person is waiting for you to explain it clearly.

Use technology in the community.

Community-facing work connects technical skill with patience, service and responsibility.

ETHS TRAVELS

Experiences should widen the student's world.

Travel and off-site experiences matter when they expose students to new people, new places, new expectations and a bigger sense of what their skills can do. The point is what returns to the clubroom.

BRINGING THE WORLD TO ETHS

The wider network should make this club stronger.

University instructors, virtual teaching and international relationships bring people into the ETHS experience who would otherwise be far away. The subject is what those connections make possible for students here.

Support assets

The support page keeps the local promise, five ways to help, and bYTCoin recognition together.

LOCAL PROMISE

Support this club. Strengthen this pipeline.

Money raised in Evanston stays in Evanston. Support is for this club and this student pipeline: ETHS students, local teaching, club equipment, student experiences, recognition, and community programs.

Fund the student pipeline.

Instruction, supplies, local programs and experiences that put students in positions of responsibility.

Donate useful equipment.

Technology and supplies that can be put to work rather than simply stored.

Bring elbow grease.

Take ownership of a defined need and help get it finished.

Connect the club.

Open a door to an instructor, employer, university, community partner or meaningful experience.

bYTCoin

Recognition system — not cryptocurrency.

A recognition layer that celebrates the student journey. It does not replace wages and it does not buy advancement on the Leadership Ladder.
