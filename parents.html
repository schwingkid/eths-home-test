For parents

Why parents love YTC.

Because it’s not just another after-school activity.

In YTC, students do more than attend meetings. They become part of a community where they are challenged, encouraged, and trusted.

They learn how to:

  • work with technology in a hands-on way,
  • solve problems with others,
  • speak up and communicate,
  • teach and mentor younger students,
  • take leadership roles,
  • and see themselves as capable of shaping the future.

This is a club where students don’t just consume opportunities.
They learn how to create them.

What makes YTC different

Skills are only the beginning.

A lot of clubs teach skills.
YTC helps students turn skills into leadership.
A lot of organizations serve young people.
YTC asks young people to serve too.
A lot of programs are adult-run.
YTC is built around students taking real responsibility — with support, guidance, and a clear path to grow.

Here, students move from Learner → Teacher → Mentor → Leader — and along the way, they help build the club for the students who come next.

Why should my child join?

Because your child deserves more than a résumé line.

They deserve a place where they can:

  • discover what they’re capable of,
  • build confidence by doing real work,
  • find friends around a shared purpose,
  • explore technology in an exciting, approachable way,
  • and become part of something bigger than themselves.

A YTC student may start out shy, unsure, or just curious.

But over time, they may become the student welcoming others at the club fair, leading a project, teaching a lesson, mentoring a younger student, or representing their school in a global exchange.

That transformation is what YTC is really about.

YTC is not only building STEM interest.

The practical answers

What it asks, who’s in the room, what it costs.

Time

As much as fits

The clubroom is open most days after school for student-run, curriculum-specific activities, plus one general meeting a week — snacks and refreshments included. Competitions, service days, and trips are optional layers on top.

Supervision

Adults present, students leading

A faculty sponsor and adult mentors are in the room. Students lead it — that balance is the design, and it has worked since 2009.

Cost

Free

No dues, nothing to buy. Equipment is donated and student-maintained. Optional travel may have separate costs and permission requirements.

This is not just a club your child joins.
